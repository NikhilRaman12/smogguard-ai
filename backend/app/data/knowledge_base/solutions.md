# Air Quality Solutions & Practical Recommendations

## Indoor Air Quality Improvement Tips

### High Effectiveness (60–95% PM2.5 Reduction)

1. **HEPA Air Purifier (80–95% reduction):** The gold standard. Choose a unit rated for your room size (look for CADR — Clean Air Delivery Rate). Run on high during pollution spikes, medium otherwise. Replace filters every 6–12 months. Budget: $100–$400.

2. **DIY Box-Fan Air Purifier (50–70% reduction):** A remarkably effective budget option. Attach a MERV-13 or higher furnace filter to the back of a standard 20" box fan using tape or bungee cords. The fan draws air through the filter, capturing fine particles. Cost: $25–$40 total. Studies from the University of Michigan show these achieve 50–70% PM2.5 reduction — comparable to commercial units costing 10x more.
   - **Build Instructions:**
     1. Purchase a 20" box fan ($15–$20) and a 20"×20" MERV-13 furnace filter ($8–$15).
     2. Place the filter on the intake side of the fan (the back). The arrow on the filter should point toward the fan.
     3. Secure with duct tape on all four edges, or use four bungee cords.
     4. Run at medium speed for best balance of filtration and noise.
     5. Replace the filter every 2–3 months (more frequently during heavy pollution).

3. **Sealed Rooms with Filtered Air (60–80% reduction):** Close all windows and doors, seal gaps with draft stoppers or wet towels, and run an air purifier. This creates a "clean room" effect.

### Moderate Effectiveness (20–50% PM2.5 Reduction)

4. **Wet Mopping & Damp Dusting (20–30% reduction):** Resuspended dust is a major source of indoor PM. Wet mop floors daily during pollution episodes instead of sweeping or dry dusting.

5. **Kitchen Exhaust Fan (25–35% reduction during cooking):** Cooking — especially frying, grilling, and gas stove use — generates significant PM2.5. Always use an exhaust fan or range hood vented to the outside.

6. **Avoid Incense, Candles, and Indoor Smoking (30–40% reduction):** A single incense stick can raise indoor PM2.5 by 50–200 µg/m³. Candles, especially scented ones, emit soot and VOCs. Eliminate these during high-AQI days.

7. **Vacuum with HEPA Filter (15–25% reduction):** Standard vacuums re-emit fine particles. HEPA-filter vacuums capture 99.97% of particles ≥0.3 µm.

8. **Close Windows During Peak Pollution (30–45% reduction):** Monitor AQI hourly. Ventilate only during low-AQI windows (typically 2–5 AM in many cities). Budget: free.

### Low Effectiveness but Supplementary (5–15% PM2.5 Reduction)

9. **Indoor Air-Purifying Plants (5–10% reduction with 5+ plants):**
   - **Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum):** Removes formaldehyde, xylene. Very hardy, thrives in indirect light.
   - **Snake Plant (Sansevieria trifasciata):** One of the few plants that converts CO₂ to O₂ at night. Excellent for bedrooms. Nearly indestructible.
   - **Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum):** Removes ammonia, benzene, formaldehyde. Blooms in low light. Mildly toxic to pets.
   - **Pothos / Money Plant (Epipremnum aureum):** Removes formaldehyde, benzene, toluene. Grows in water or soil, low maintenance.
   - **Areca Palm (Dypsis lutescens):** NASA's top-rated air purifier. Excellent natural humidifier. Needs bright indirect light.
   - **Boston Fern (Nephrolepis exaltata):** Best natural formaldehyde remover. Needs humidity and indirect light.
   - **Rubber Plant (Ficus elastica):** Effective against formaldehyde. Easy to maintain.
   - **Aloe Vera:** Removes formaldehyde and benzene. Doubles as a medicinal plant. Needs bright light.
   - *Note:* While NASA's Clean Air Study popularised plant-based air purification, independent studies show that the effect in real rooms is modest. Plants are best used as a supplement to mechanical filtration, not a replacement.

10. **Salt Lamps & Beeswax Candles (negligible direct effect):** Despite marketing claims, salt lamps do not measurably reduce PM2.5. Beeswax candles produce fewer pollutants than paraffin but still emit some PM. Not recommended as air purification.

## Low-Emission Transportation

11. **Public Transit (60–80% CO₂ reduction vs. private car):** A bus carrying 40 passengers produces ~4.5 kg CO₂ per km, but split across passengers, that is only 0.089 kg per person per km versus 0.21 kg for a solo car trip. Metro/subway is even better at 0.041 kg CO₂/passenger-km.

12. **Cycling & Walking (100% emission reduction):** Zero direct emissions. Health benefits include 30% reduced cardiovascular disease risk. When AQI > 150, switch to indoor exercise or wear an N95 mask while cycling.

13. **Carpooling (40–60% reduction per person):** Sharing a ride with 2–3 others cuts per-person emissions proportionally. Use apps like BlaBlaCar or local rideshare groups.

14. **Electric Vehicles (70–85% lifecycle reduction):** Even accounting for electricity generation, EVs produce 50–70% fewer emissions than petrol cars over their lifetime. In regions with clean grids, savings exceed 85%.

15. **Remote Work (100% commute elimination):** Each work-from-home day saves an average of 4.6 kg CO₂ for a 20 km round-trip car commute.

## Sustainable Cooking & Heating

16. **Induction Cooking (70% CO₂ reduction vs. gas):** Gas stoves emit NO₂ and PM2.5 indoors — a Stanford study found they raise indoor NO₂ above WHO limits in poorly ventilated kitchens. Induction is faster, cleaner, and more energy-efficient.

17. **Electric Space Heaters vs. Wood Burning (90% PM2.5 reduction):** A single wood-burning stove can emit as much PM2.5 as 1,000 electric heaters. If wood heat is necessary, use EPA-certified stoves and dry, seasoned hardwood.

18. **Pressure Cooker / Instant Pot (50% energy reduction):** Cooks food 70% faster than conventional methods, reducing gas/electricity use and indoor pollutant exposure.

## Community & Advocacy Actions

19. **Report Polluting Activities:** Use local pollution reporting apps or government hotlines to report illegal burning, industrial emissions, or construction dust violations.

20. **Community Air Quality Monitoring:** Affordable sensors (PurpleAir, AirGradient) cost $50–$250 and can be placed on balconies to create hyperlocal air quality maps.

21. **Support Urban Green Spaces:** Trees reduce local PM by 7–24%. Advocate for tree planting in your neighbourhood, especially along busy roads.

22. **School Zone Protection:** Campaign for anti-idling zones near schools, green barriers (hedges reduce PM by 30–60%), and relocated bus queuing areas.
